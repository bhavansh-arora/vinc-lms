# Hindi glossary for VINC Lab

Every Hindi translation in VINC Lab follows this list, so the same idea always has the same word.

## Style

- **Who reads it.** Children in Grades 6–8, their parents, and teachers in Indian schools. Write the
  simple, everyday Hindi of a good NCERT Hindi-medium textbook. Do not use heavy Sanskrit-based words
  when a common word exists, and do not write Hinglish in Latin letters.
- **Script.** Devanagari throughout. Digits stay 0–9 (as in CBSE papers): `कक्षा 6`, `3 पीरियड`, `₹ 20`.
- **Address.** Speak to students with **तुम** (warm, like a teacher in class: "अपने समूह के साथ बैठो").
  Speak to parents, teachers and coordinators with **आप** ("अपना पासवर्ड बदलें"). Buttons and menu items
  use the short polite form: **सहेजें**, **जोड़ें**, **हटाएँ**, **बदलें**, **खोलें**, **लॉग इन करें**.
- **Common English words.** Where Indian students and teachers normally say the English word, write it
  in Devanagari: कंप्यूटर, मोबाइल, पासवर्ड, ईमेल, पोर्टफोलियो, क्विज़, लैब, सर्किट, बैटरी, LED, सेंसर, रोबोट,
  ऐप, वीडियो, फ़ोटो, पोस्टर, चार्ट, मॉडल, प्रोजेक्ट. Keep these in Latin letters: VINC Lab, NCERT, CBSE,
  Scratch, Arduino, LED, QR, UPI, AI, and anything people type (User IDs, codes, emails).
- **Names.** Keep people's names and transliterate them: Ramesh ji → रमेश जी, Kamla Dadi → कमला दादी.
  Hindi words already written in English go back to Devanagari: baingan → बैंगन, mali → माली.
- **Punctuation.** End sentences with the purna viram **।** (questions with **?**, exclamations with **!**).
  Keep the same placeholders exactly as they are: `{n}`, `{name}`, `$1`, and any HTML tags such as
  `<b>…</b>` (move them to the right words, but keep every tag).
- **Length.** Hindi runs about 20–30% longer than English. Keep button and menu labels short.
